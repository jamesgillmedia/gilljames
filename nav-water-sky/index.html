how it feels
You touch the night water and fall into the sky it was reflecting. The work is up among the stars, and the moon is how you get home.
1
Reflected sky: the 7 constellations and ᑕᐣᓯ are drawn into the water, mirrored and wobbling. Each one is also a real link in the HTML.
2
Ripple hole: the water shader opens a circle where you tapped. The stars show through.
3
The dive: a 0.6 s fall with the surface overhead, landing on /sky/ (or straight on the cluster you tapped).
4
Cluster = section page. /film/ opens with the camera on the film stars. The normal list scrolls underneath.
5
Star → Scope player, opened from that star. The sky freezes.
6
Rise: the small moon (or back) reverses the dive. The visited cluster glows in the reflection.

Deep links, solved by design: each page knows its own camera spot (sky position + zoom) and starts there. Nothing depends on the history of the visit. zoom.js's in-memory state only ever moves you between real URLs.
DFALLBACKS
the sky never hides the work
No WebGL / no JS. The home shows the water poster with the reflected constellations as a CSS layer. The dive becomes a CSS circle reveal (view transition) or a plain page change. /sky/ is a static SVG map, and every cluster and star is a link. Section pages are complete lists.
Reduced motion. No ripple, dive, flying or twinkle. Pages change instantly, the sky is a still map, the player waits for a tap, and no 1080p plays on phones.
Slow phone. The water's FPS probe (under 30 fps for 1.5 s) swaps in the poster. zoom.js depth turns itself off over 34 ms frames, and the sky drops to one still image.
